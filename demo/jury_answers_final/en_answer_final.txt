An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is a financial operation in which a company makes part of its share capital available to the public in exchange for newly raised funds. It allows the company to solicit public savings through a public offering of securities, subject to legal and regulatory obligations intended to protect investors. These include publishing a prospectus and providing ongoing financial and extra-financial information.

There are two main types of IPO, along with a mixed structure:

- **Capital increase:** The company creates new shares and raises new capital from the market, providing an additional source of financing for its development.
- **Sale of existing shares:** Original shareholders sell some of their existing shares to new investors. The company receives no new liquidity; instead, the original shareholders reduce their participation in its capital.
- **Mixed operation:** This combines a capital increase with the sale of existing shares.
